## Design Context

### Users
NoTerm is used by operations engineers and software engineers working with real servers and local shells. They typically open the product while troubleshooting incidents, inspecting runtime state, managing remote systems, or moving quickly between sessions. Most users are intermediate to advanced, but the interface should still lower friction in stressful moments.

### Brand Personality
Calm, professional, friendly. The product should feel reliable under pressure, never noisy or theatrical. The AI assistant should read like a helpful teammate: approachable in tone, precise in guidance, and grounded in the current terminal context.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a quiet desktop-tool aesthetic rather than a flashy chat-product aesthetic. Favor clarity, restraint, and trust over novelty. The UI should feel like a focused operator workspace: light surfaces, clear hierarchy, compact but breathable spacing, and subtle emphasis that supports action without stealing attention from the terminal.

### Design Principles
1. Prioritize the next useful action over feature breadth.
2. Keep AI grounded in current terminal context and server state whenever possible.
3. Make high-stress workflows feel calmer through clear hierarchy and concise copy.
4. Use progressive disclosure for secondary analysis, summaries, and advanced controls.
5. Match desktop utility expectations: fast, direct, readable, and never decorative for its own sake.

### Design System Source Of Truth
Use `docs/design-system.md` as the operating specification for all future UI work.
Design passes such as `normalize`, `typeset`, `arrange`, `polish`, `clarify`, and
related refinements must follow that document before introducing new patterns.
